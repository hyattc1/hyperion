# Practical lessons from existing poker bot competitions (MIT Pokerbots, ACPC, CMU, others) for the 2026 Sequoia "No Limit" tournament

Research date: 2026-10-31. Sources are 2018–2026 unless marked older. "Confirmed" items are read directly from engine source, official rules PDFs, or official pages; inferences are listed separately.

## Key Question 0 (context): What is the 2026 Sequoia "No Limit" tournament actually, and how does it differ from MIT/ACPC conventions?

### Takeaway
No Limit (Sequoia, Citadel Securities, OpenAI) is a 60-minute, Python-only, 8-max sit-and-go format with a 1 ms per-decision limit, ICM payouts and escalating blinds, i.e. closer to a short-stack tournament than to the heads-up, fixed-stack, 1,000-hand cash format every MIT and CMU participant is used to. Fold-or-check on any error/timeout and a weighted T1..T6 scoring make "ship a crash-free bot early, then iterate every 5 minutes" the dominant meta.

### Cited Findings
- Format: "Teams of 1 or 2 get 60 minutes to build a pokerbot from scratch"; bots "play thousands of games across six scored rounds"; open to current students and recent grads ('25/'26); regionals Bay Area Nov 6, Boston Nov 9, Pittsburgh Nov 12; finals San Francisco Nov 15, 2026 — [Sequoia official page](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/); [Digg coverage](https://digg.com/ai/smdduv3c)
- Official rules (Sec. 3): 60 minutes to build "a Python bot that implements the provided Agent interface"; starter kit = agent template, local simulator, ICM calculator, payout structure and blind schedule; "Each bot must return a decision within one millisecond"; max file size 100 MB; a bot that errors or exceeds the time limit "automatically folds, or checks if checking is available"; bots run in sandboxes with no network access; one submission every five minutes, most recent accepted submission is active; Astra and Codex may be used during the build phase, but AI tools may not make live gameplay decisions — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)
- Scoring (Sec. 4): six scored tournaments T1–T6, T1 at end of build phase, later ones 20 minutes apart; active bot frozen at start of each; each tournament = 10,000 eight-bot sit-and-gos with random seating; a table ends when one bot has all chips or after 200 hands; 2,000 starting chips; blinds start 10/20 and increase every 25 hands; points by finishing position (distribution announced at build start); at the 200-hand cap remaining points split by ICM; empty seats filled by random copies of submitted bots that earn no points; final score = weighted sum, T1 x1 ... T6 x6, each tournament score being average points per table; ties broken by a skill-based tiebreaker challenge — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)
- Sec. 3.8: bot state resets at the start of each table; opponents are anonymized so bots cannot identify specific teams — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)
- Prizes: top 5 teams per regional get $5,000 per team and advance; up to five wildcards; finals $100K / $50K / $25K — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)
- The landing page's exhibits describe NLHE with blinds 50/100, a heads-up exhibit resetting stacks to 10,000 every hand, and a "message-trace" exhibit showing a dealer exchanging messages with bots including think time — [Sequoia official page](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/). These exhibit parameters differ from the rules' 2,000 chips / 10/20 blinds; treat the rules page as authoritative.
- Third-party summary: "the poker variant remains confidential until each event's build phase begins"; its own advice is to get a valid bot working early because a working baseline competes while logic improves — [Zeniteq](https://www.zeniteq.com/sequoia-and-openai-open-a-100k-pokerbot-tournament-6aycef)
- Applicants can do an optional 3-minute "Castle Challenge" (Blotto: 100 soldiers across 5 castles worth 1–5) for priority registration — [Sequoia official page](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/). MIT Pokerbots 2026 ran a similar Blotto side challenge (10 integers summing to 1000 over stacks worth 1–10; ties award nothing) — [pokerbots.org/blotto](https://pokerbots.org/blotto)

### Inferences
- 1 ms per decision rules out any online search/solving (MIT's 2024 GTO Wizard entry already called 30 s / 1,000 hands "only a few milliseconds per action" and built a depth-limited solver around it; 1 ms is an order of magnitude tighter). Everything must be table lookup or closed-form arithmetic at runtime; anything heavier must be precomputed in the 60-minute window or hand-authored.
- With 2,000 chips, 10/20 blinds rising every 25 hands and a 200-hand cap, effective stacks fall to roughly 10–20 BB within a few blind levels (exact schedule is in the starter kit). Push/fold charts and ICM-aware shoving/calling thresholds likely matter more than postflop play; MIT-style fixed 200 BB cash-game CFR is the wrong prior.
- State reset per table (max 200 hands, 8 players) means opponent modeling must converge in tens of observations per opponent; Beta-smoothed VPIP/PFR/aggression counters (as in the MIT 2024 abh-win bot) are the right scale, not per-opponent learned models.
- Because T6 is weighted 6x and T1 1x, and submissions land every 5 minutes, the expected-value play is a safe baseline for T1–T2 and aggressive iteration for T4–T6, with a tested fallback kept active.
- Filler bots are random copies of other teams' submissions, so the field is partly self-similar; exploits that target weak submissions (calling stations, random bots) pay in early tournaments.

### Gaps
- Exact point distribution per finishing position, blind schedule, and the Agent interface signature are not public before the build phase.
- Whether the sandbox allows numpy or any third-party package is not stated in public rules.

## Key Question 1: MIT Pokerbots — variants per year (2019–2026), engine, languages, time banks, match format, and what winning teams did

### Takeaway
MIT Pokerbots is a heads-up, 1,000-hand, stacks-reset-every-hand, 1/2-blind format with a 30 s (2020–2024) or 60 s (2025–2026) total clock per match, Python/Java/C++ skeletons, and a new twist each January. Winners and strong entries pair precomputed equity/strength tables with opponent profiling; the organizers themselves say exploitative, heuristic-heavy bots tend to win, and full CFR is a minority approach constrained by the clock.

### Cited Findings

Variants and engine parameters by year (read from each year's `config.py` / `engine.py` / `variant.pdf`):
- 2020: 1,000 rounds, 200-chip stacks, blinds 1/2, `STARTING_GAME_CLOCK = 30.`; the engine evaluates hands through a hidden `PERM` mapping of card values (ranks are secretly permuted; the board and hole cards are shown both raw and mapped in the log) — [engine-2020 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2020/master/config.py); [engine-2020 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2020/master/engine.py)
- 2021: `NUM_BOARDS = 3`, 500 rounds, 200-chip stacks, 30 s clock; the protocol adds an `AssignAction` ('A') where the bot allocates its hole cards across three boards and each board has its own betting; socket clauses are prefixed per board (`#F`, `#R###`, `#B...`) — [engine-2021 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2021/master/config.py); [engine-2021 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2021/master/engine.py). A 2021 participant's repo includes a "Blotto Hold'em.pdf", consistent with the 3-board allocation variant — [ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot](https://github.com/ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot)
- 2022: "swap" variant: at the flop each hole card is independently replaced from the deck with probability `FLOP_PERCENT = 0.1`, and at the turn with `TURN_PERCENT = 0.05`; the protocol adds a `U**,**` clause for the updated hand; 1,000 rounds, 200 stacks, 30 s — [engine-2022 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2022/master/config.py); [engine-2022 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2022/master/engine.py)
- 2023: stacks raised to 400; the round state carries a `final_street` and `reached_run`, and the deck is advanced while the would-be final card's suit is a heart or diamond (eval7 suit index 1 or 2), i.e. a River-of-Blood style "keep dealing while the river is red" rule; showdown "is no longer guaranteed to be street == 5" — [engine-2023 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2023/master/config.py); [engine-2023 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2023/master/engine.py). The home-game rule "if the river card is red, another river card is dealt ... until a black river card appears" is described at [onlinepokersitesus.com](https://www.onlinepokersitesus.com/poker-rules/poker-variants/); the MIT engine's exact payout handling when a player is all-in before the run (`reached_run`) is in the engine source.
- 2024: Auction Hold'em: after the flop both players submit a sealed bid for a third hole card, second-price (winner pays the loser's bid into the pot), ties give both a card; 1,000 hands, 400 stacks, 1/2 blinds, 30 s clock; 1 GB RAM and 100 MB compressed storage caps — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024); [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot); [engine-2024 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2024/master/config.py)
- 2025: "No-Limit Bounty Hold'em": every 25 rounds each player is privately assigned a bounty rank; a winner who "hits" the bounty (rank in their hole cards or on the board) gets `1.5 * normal winnings + 10`, where normal winnings = chips the opponent put in; split pots with exactly one bounty hit pay x/4 + 10; rounding favors the out-of-position player; 1,000 rounds, 400 stacks, 1/2 blinds — [class-resources-2025 variant.pdf](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/class-resources-2025/blob/main/variant.pdf); `STARTING_GAME_CLOCK = 60.0`, `ROUNDS_PER_BOUNTY = 25`, `BOUNTY_RATIO = 1.5`, `BOUNTY_CONSTANT = 10`, `PLAYER_TIMEOUT = 120` — [engine-2025 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2025/master/config.py)
- 2026: "Toss or Hold'em": 3 hole cards preflop; flop is 2 cards; then a discard round (out-of-position player discards first, dealer sees that card before choosing) in which each player moves one hole card face-up to the board, giving 4 board cards; turn and river as normal so 6 board cards at showdown, best 5-card hand; 1,000 rounds, 400 stacks, 1/2 blinds, 60 s clock; streets encoded 0,2,3,4,5,6 (preflop, BB discard, SB discard, flop betting, turn, river); hand evaluation moved from eval7 to a custom `pkrbot 1.0.4` library and setup uses `uv` — [class-resources-2026 variant.pdf](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/class-resources-2026/blob/main/variant.pdf); [engine-2026 README and config](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026); [python_skeleton player.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026/master/python_skeleton/player.py)
- 2019 and earlier: not covered by the current GitHub org (oldest engine repo is 2020). A 2017 winning entry was a CFR bot with hand bucketing and a discard mechanic in the helper code, so 2017 was a discard variant — [Fro116/Pokerbots](https://github.com/Fro116/Pokerbots) (2017; older than the 2018 cutoff, included for lineage). The IAP 2018 competition was cancelled — [The Tech, Dec 2017](https://thetech.com/2017/12/07/pokerbot-cancelled)

Engine, protocol, languages, clocks:
- Every year ships Python, Java and C++ skeletons; the engine is Python (`python3 engine.py`, configured by `config.py`); C++ needs C++17 + Boost (+ fmt in 2021–2024); Java 8+; Windows users must run under WSL — [engine-2021 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2021/master/README.md); [engine-2026 README](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026). No Rust skeleton exists in any year's repo — [mitpokerbots org repositories](https://github.com/orgs/mitpokerbots/repositories)
- Wire protocol is a line-oriented socket format: `T#.###` game clock, `P#` player index, `H**,**` hand, `F/C/K/R###` actions, `B` board, `O` opponent hand, `D###` delta, `Q` game over; clauses separated by spaces, messages end with `\n`; the engine expects `K` as an ack at the end of each round — [engine-2020 engine.py comments](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2020/master/engine.py)
- Clock enforcement: the engine subtracts wall time of each bot response from `game_clock` when `ENFORCE_GAME_CLOCK` is true, and logs "<name> ran out of time" and sets the clock to 0 when it goes negative; `BUILD_TIMEOUT = 10 s`, `CONNECT_TIMEOUT = 10 s`; in 2025–2026 a per-message socket `PLAYER_TIMEOUT = 120 s` also exists — [engine-2026 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026/master/engine.py); [engine-2026 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026/master/config.py)
- Student newspaper (Feb 2024): "Students have to be able to play 1000 hands in thirty seconds, so it isn't feasible to just copy a bot found online"; the variant changes every year and the engine is homemade; the biggest challenge for most students was the limits of the homemade engine, not poker experience — [The Tech, "Meet Alex Zhang, President of Pokerbots"](https://thetech.com/2024/02/08/pokerbots)

Course and tournament structure:
- Course 6.9630, teams of 1–4, ~12 lectures over IAP, a scrimmage server running all month, a mini tournament at the end of each of the first three weeks, a final tournament; a "Week 1 Bot" is due Friday of week 1; pass/fail based on scrimmage participation and a final strategy report — [class-resources-2025 syllabus.pdf](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/class-resources-2025/blob/main/syllabus.pdf); [class-resources-2026 syllabus.pdf](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/class-resources-2026/blob/main/syllabus.pdf)
- 2025 lecture order: Intro; Probability and Statistics; Poker Strategy; Game Theory; Machine Learning; Reinforcement Learning (recitations: environment setup, NumPy/stats packages). 2026: Intro; Probability and Statistics; Poker Strategy; Machine Learning; Game Theory — same syllabi
- Official reference bots published per year: 2024 lecture bots "applying pot odds", "applying game theory", "with precomputed strengths"; 2025 references include "Exploitative bandit bot", a "CFR demo", a PyTorch neural-net demo and a logistic-regression demo; 2026 has three reference bots (a participant's repo labels them toss-smallest, good-cards, Monte Carlo) plus a "playground" to play against your own bot — [mitpokerbots org repositories](https://github.com/orgs/mitpokerbots/repositories); [juanorbea/pokerbot](https://github.com/juanorbea/pokerbot)
- Prizes exceed $50,000 (2026) with sponsors HRT, Jump, Citadel, DRW, Jane Street, BAM, D.E. Shaw, Five Rings, SIG, Two Sigma; game and tournament structure are secret until the January unveiling; Python, Java or C++ — [pokerbots.org](https://pokerbots.org/)
- A third-party blog states the final is a double-elimination bracket; this is a secondary source and is not confirmed by MIT material — [openpoker.ai](https://openpoker.ai/blog/best-ai-poker-platforms)
- The Tech (2024): bots "that exploit opponent weaknesses, especially those using the most poker theory and heuristics, tend to perform best"; a past all-in bot "was hard to counter in real time", and after the strategy was discussed in lectures similar bots underperformed; hands with high variance from future cards (draws) "typically outperform their equity" — [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2024/02/08/pokerbots)

What strong entries did:
- GTO Wizard AI (2024, ~5 days of work): C++17 core with Cython bindings; depth-limited subgame solving at each decision with an equity-based "all-in values" heuristic at the depth limit (which they call "not considered to be a good heuristic"); a time manager allocating the 30 s across streets by predefined ratios and expected remaining actions; OMPEval-based evaluator at ~400M hands/s; precomputed 1755 x 1326 "3v2 - 2v3" equity-difference matrix for the auction; bids scaled by 1.8x the equity-based bid outperformed 0.5–2.5x; they note an abstraction + MCCFR blueprint "would likely have been optimal under the time limit" but they chose an approach that scales with compute; placement not reported — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- abh-win (2024, C++): Beta-smoothed VPIP/PFR/3-bet/aggression/fold-to-bet/WTSD stats classify the opponent as Nit/TAG/LAG/Calling station/Maniac and set open ranges and value/bluff behavior; bounded Monte Carlo equity vs an estimated range with a per-estimate time cap; marginal-EV bidding against the opponent's empirical bid distribution. 19–31 ns per hand eval, ~170 µs per 1,500-sample equity estimate, 160–235 µs per decision; used ~1.6 s of the 30 s clock over 1,000 hands with zero state mismatches. Profiling roughly tripled win rate vs a calling station (+1008 to +2861 BB/100), turned an all-in bidder from +61 to +2002, and made no measurable difference vs TAG or Nit — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)
- 2025 participant: bounty variant bot combining CFR and RL strategies with a bandit that picks the best-performing strategy per opponent and real-time opponent modeling; placement not stated — [spei04 project page](https://spei04.github.io/projects/pokerbots/)
- 2021 participant: "played millions of simulated pokergames and precomputed poker-hand strengths before the tournament"; tight-aggressive with added randomness "so other bots couldn't predict and exploit"; beat the staff "Difficult" bot and ranked above average vs 4-person teams — [ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot](https://github.com/ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot)
- 2017 prize winner (SPAM): C++ bot, Python-generated win-rate tables (river first, then turn averaged over 46 rivers, discard choice baked into the turn table); decision by win-rate interval; after scrimmage they tightened against all-in bots, became wary of very large raises, and bet smaller for value against CFR bots "which tended to fold to large raises" — [sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017](https://github.com/sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017) (2017)
- 2017 champion: CFR-trained bot with state abstraction and off-tree action handling, offline training separated from a runtime player with precomputed helper tables (hand bucketing, discard logic) — [Fro116/Pokerbots](https://github.com/Fro116/Pokerbots) (2017)
- Older MIT entry (placed 2nd in the new-team bracket): tracked opponent statistics to adjust aggression and looseness; skipped precise odds because Monte Carlo error "was small and not their bottleneck"; preferred a "thinking" bot over a lookup-table "reading" bot — [jlrubin MIT page](https://www.mit.edu/~jlrubin/projects/pokerbots) (undated, pre-2018)

### Inferences
- Across six years the fixed scaffolding (heads-up, stacks reset each hand, 1/2 blinds, 1,000 hands, one total clock) is stable while the twist changes; MIT alumni will arrive at the Sequoia Boston leg with reflexes for "precompute strength tables for the twist, profile the opponent, stay far under the clock." The Sequoia 8-max SNG with escalating blinds breaks the first reflex (stacks are not reset, ICM matters).
- The MIT clock is generous relative to Sequoia: 30–60 s / 1,000 hands is ~30–60 ms per hand, versus 1 ms per decision. The abh-win numbers (160–235 µs per decision in C++) show that Monte Carlo equity with ~1,500 samples fits a 1 ms budget only in a compiled language; in Python it will not, so Sequoia bots need precomputed preflop equity/strength tables and cheap postflop proxies.
- The clear pattern in MIT write-ups is that opponent profiling buys little against solid opponents but enormous amounts against calling stations, maniacs and all-in bots, which are common in short student fields.

### Gaps
- No official MIT page lists past winners or final placements for 2019–2026; no winner write-up for 2019–2023 was found.
- 2019 variant and format are unknown (no engine repo for 2019 in the org).
- The exact final tournament format (round robin vs bracket, divisions) is not published; "double elimination" comes from a secondary blog only.
- 2025/2026 `Lectures/` slide contents could not be read here; only titles from the syllabus.

## Key Question 2: ACPC — protocol, winners in HUNL and multiplayer, exploitative vs equilibrium, time rules

### Takeaway
ACPC (2006–2018) used a TCP text protocol (`MATCHSTATE:<seat>:<hand#>:<betting>:<cards>` with `f/c/r<amount>` replies), round-robin duplicate matches of 3,000 hands, memory wiped per match, and an average of 7 s per hand (2 s in the 2018 six-player event). Late HUNL winners were equilibrium-approximating CFR bots (Slumbot 2018, Baby Tartanian8 2016); the "total bankroll" division was explicitly designed to reward exploitation and the "instant run-off" division to reward defensive game-theoretic play.

### Cited Findings
- Format (2012 description by an Alberta CPRG member): three games (HU limit, HU no-limit, 3-player limit); two-player events use round robin where "several 3000-hand matches are played between every pair of programs ... with each program's memory being wiped clean before each match"; three-player uses 1,000-hand matches per triple; duplicate matches with identical deals and swapped seats; programs "average seven seconds per hand to act"; two winner rules per game: total bankroll ("rewards exploiting opponents' weaknesses") and bankroll instant run-off (eliminates the lowest stack iteratively; rewards protecting your stack) — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-texas-hold-em-tournament-for-ais)
- 2018 call for participation: heads-up no-limit plus, for the first time, six-player no-limit; Amazon EC2 instances ("quite a bit less powerful than many desktop machines"); 250 GB maximum submission size; six-player "only 2 seconds per hand will be allowed rather than 7"; Noam Brown incoming chair — [computerpokercompetition.org](http://www.computerpokercompetition.org/)
- Results: 2018 heads-up winner Slumbot, six-player winner PokerBot5; 2017 winner in both competitions Intermission (Unfold Poker) — [computerpokercompetition.org](http://www.computerpokercompetition.org/). 2016: Baby Tartanian8 (Brown and Sandholm) first in HUNL total bankroll and third in instant run-off — [CMU CSD news](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/news/carnegie-mellon-pokerbot-extends-hot-streak-at-computer-poker-contest); [BabyTartanian8 IJCAI demo paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/BabyTartanian8.ijcai16demo.pdf). 2014: Tartanian7 won both HUNL categories; 2010: Tartanian4 (TBR) and HyperboreanNL (IRO) — [Alberta CPRG news](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/news_2010.html)
- Slumbot (Eric Jackson) is described as "champion of the 2018 ACPC" and the strongest openly available HUNL agent; it is an equilibrium-approximation (CFR) bot — [DecisionHoldem paper, arXiv 2201.11580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11580)
- Protocol: server sends e.g. `MATCHSTATE:0:30:rc/:9s8h|/8c8d5c` (seat, hand number, betting string by street separated by `/`, cards with `|` separating players' hole cards and `/` separating board streets); the client echoes the state and appends its action, e.g. `...:c`; raises carry amounts like `r300r900c` — [poker-ai.org ACPC protocol explainer](https://poker-ai.org/demystifying-poker-ai-the-role-of-acpc-protocol/); full spec v2.0.0 (Oct 2010) — [ACPC protocol.pdf](http://www.computerpokercompetition.org/downloads/documents/protocols/protocol.pdf)
- Dealer: `./dealer matchName <game file> <hands> <seed> Alice Bob` prints one port per seat; players connect with exactly two args (IP, port); dealer startup prints `--t_response 10000 --t_hand 600000 --t_per_hand 6000` (milliseconds; per-response, per-hand, and per-hand-average budgets); game definition files specify `stack`, `blind`, `numplayers`, `numrounds`, `numholecards`, `numboardcards` etc. — [acpc-server mirror README](https://github.com/crissilvaeng/acpc-server)
- ACPC itself ran 2006–2018 — [HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Software:Computer_poker_player)
- 683 heads-up ACPC match logs (2010–2017) have been packaged as an open dataset — [irw PR](https://github.com/ben-domingue/irw/pull/3255)

### Inferences
- ACPC rewarded heavy offline compute (250 GB blueprints, 7 s/hand) and equilibrium play; its winners' methods (abstraction + CFR blueprints, subgame solving) do not transfer to a 60-minute, 1 ms, 100 MB setting except as intuition about ranges and bet-size abstractions.
- The TBR vs IRO split is the clearest institutional statement that "exploit the field" and "be unexploitable" are different objectives; Sequoia's points-per-table SNG scoring with filler copies of weak bots is a total-bankroll-style objective, which favors exploitation.

### Gaps
- Per-hand time limits for ACPC years before 2017 were not found in primary sources (only the 7 s average from the 2012 article and 2017/2018 calls).
- No ACPC 3-player no-limit event existed; the 2018 six-player NLHE results (PokerBot5) have no accessible write-up found here.

## Key Question 3: CMU — student-facing poker bot competitions and courses; what Pittsburgh participants will know

### Takeaway
CMU has no Sandholm-run student bot league, but Pittsburgh has two relevant student events: the CMU Data Science Club's Jump-Trading-sponsored AI Poker Tournament (Python 3.12, heads-up 1,000-hand matches with fresh bankroll each hand, ELO ladder, secret variant revealed at kickoff, Gym-style engine) and a one-day "Poker AI @ CMU" build event in 2024. Graduate exposure comes from Sandholm's 15-888 Computational Game Solving (CFR, Libratus, subgame solving), so Pittsburgh teams skew toward game-theory-literate but with the same heads-up/fixed-stack habits as MIT teams.

### Cited Findings
- CMU AI Poker Tournament (CMU Data Science Club, sponsored by Jump Trading; page footer 2026): teams of 1–2 CMU students; Python only (3.12); heads-up matches of 1,000 hands, each hand starts with a fresh bankroll, highest total net gain wins; variant revealed at the opening ceremony and "more complex than standard no-limit Texas Hold 'em"; development framework with docs and example bots, engine implements the OpenAI Gym interface with example RL code promised; online phase of ~20 automatic matches per team per day plus limited extra challenge matches vs higher-ELO opponents; highest-ELO teams advance to a live final; approved external library list; $6,000 prizes ($3K/$2K/$1K); a Poker/Game Theory workshop covering poker, game theory and basic RL — [cmudsc.com/pokerai-2026](https://cmudsc.com/pokerai-2026/)
- "Poker AI @ CMU" (March 2024, Doherty Hall): participants wrote Python bots in a sandbox to play heads-up poker in a single day — search-result summary of [CMU student affairs calendar](https://tockify.com/carnegie.mellon.student.affairs/detail/2940/1711807200000) (the page body did not render when fetched; treat details as unverified)
- CMU Poker Club exists and is sponsored by Jane Street and Jump Trading — [CMU Poker Club on X](https://x.com/CMUPokerClub) (search snippet; page had no posts)
- 15-888 Computational Game Solving (Sandholm; offered F23, F24, F25, F26): lecture course ending in student projects (individual or groups of 2–3), lectures cover Libratus and the history of poker AI; F26 project proposals were due Oct 9 with presentations in Nov–Dec; final project 50%, exam 20% — [cs15-888F26](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-888F26/); [cs15-888F25](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-888F25/); [cs15-888F24](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-888F24/)

### Inferences
- The Pittsburgh regional on Nov 12 falls during 15-888's project season, so some entrants will have fresh CFR/subgame-solving knowledge; the CMU DSC event's Python-only, heads-up, 1,000-hand, fresh-bankroll format mirrors MIT's, so Pittsburgh participants share the MIT habits (and the same mismatch with Sequoia's 8-max SNG).
- No evidence of any CMU student event using multiplayer tables or ICM; that is unfamiliar ground for both Boston and Pittsburgh communities.

### Gaps
- No write-ups, winners or engine repos from the CMU DSC tournament or the 2024 Poker AI @ CMU event were found.
- No evidence of a Sandholm-group student competition; 15-888 is a research-project course.

## Key Question 4: Cross-competition lessons — time budgeting, robustness, exploit vs GTO, precomputation, testing harnesses, language

### Takeaway
Every source converges: never time out or crash (the engine folds for you), precompute strength/equity tables so runtime is table lookups, instrument a few opponent stats and adjust aggression/looseness, test with a local harness over tens of thousands of hands with confidence intervals, and use a compiled language only if you can afford it; in a 60-minute Python-only contest, the equivalent is NumPy-free lookup tables and arithmetic.

### Cited Findings
(a) Time budgeting
- MIT: 30 s (2020–2024) or 60 s (2025–2026) for 1,000 hands, deducted per response; running out logs "ran out of time" — [engine-2026 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2026/master/engine.py); [engine configs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2024/master/config.py)
- GTO Wizard's time manager split the clock across streets with fixed ratios times expected remaining actions — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- abh-win capped each equity estimate with a time budget and finished 1,000 hands in ~1.6 s of a 30 s clock — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)
- Sequoia: 1 ms per decision, hard — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)
- Other student contests use cumulative banks: IIIT Bangalore 2026 event "~60 seconds for 400 hands" with a hard wall-clock kill — [Unstop listing](https://unstop.com/hackathons/pokerbots-synergy-26-international-institute-of-information-technology-iiit-bangalore-1762863)

(b) Robustness
- Sequoia: error or timeout folds (or checks) — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html); an HN-discussed LLM poker tournament used the same rule (a timeout makes the player fold) — [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45730094)
- MIT engine kills the bot build after 10 s and the connection after 10 s; illegal actions are logged and replaced (2021: "attempted illegal assignment") — [engine-2021 engine.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2021/master/engine.py); [engine-2020 config.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mitpokerbots/engine-2020/master/config.py)
- The Tech: most students struggled with "the limits of the homemade engine" more than with poker — [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2024/02/08/pokerbots)
- abh-win reports "0 state mismatches" over 1,000 hands as a shipped metric — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)

(c) Exploit vs GTO
- The Tech: exploitative, heuristic-heavy bots tend to win MIT; an all-in bot once dominated until it was taught in lecture — [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2024/02/08/pokerbots)
- abh-win: profiling worth ~3x vs calling station and ~+2000 BB/100 vs an all-in bidder, ~0 vs TAG/Nit — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)
- SPAM 2017: vs CFR bots "which tended to fold to large raises," bet smaller for value — [SPAM_pokerbot_2017](https://github.com/sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017)
- MIT 2025 published an "Exploitative bandit bot" as an official reference, and a 2025 team used a bandit over CFR/RL strategies per opponent — [mitpokerbots repositories](https://github.com/orgs/mitpokerbots/repositories); [spei04](https://spei04.github.io/projects/pokerbots/)
- ACPC institutionalized the split: total bankroll rewards exploitation, instant run-off rewards defense — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-texas-hold-em-tournament-for-ais)
- Randomization as anti-exploitation: 2021 MIT entrant added randomness "so other bots couldn't predict and exploit its play" — [ngnnah](https://github.com/ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot)
- Practitioner guide: "Equity plus pot odds plus some model of implied odds produces a solid, tight, exploitable player"; start with heuristics, equity and live testing before deep CFR — [openpoker.ai Pluribus explainer](https://openpoker.ai/blog/pluribus-poker-bot-explained)

(d) Precomputed ranges and equity tables
- 2017 SPAM: river win-rate tables first, turn as average over 46 rivers, flop/preflop likewise; Python generated, C++ consumed — [SPAM_pokerbot_2017](https://github.com/sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017)
- 2021 entrant: millions of simulated games precomputed before the tournament — [ngnnah](https://github.com/ngnnah/python_AutonomousPokerbot)
- GTO Wizard 2024: precomputed 1755 x 1326 equity-difference matrix for the auction decision; OMPEval evaluator — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- MIT's own 2024 Lecture 4 reference bot is "Sample bot with precomputed strengths" — [mitpokerbots repositories](https://github.com/orgs/mitpokerbots/repositories)
- Jeremy Rubin (older MIT entry): Monte Carlo error was small and "not their bottleneck" — [jlrubin](https://www.mit.edu/~jlrubin/projects/pokerbots)

(e) Testing harnesses
- MIT provides the engine, scrimmage server, weekly mini tournaments, a "playground" to play your bot by hand (`player_chatbot`), and reference bots — [syllabus 2025](https://github.com/mitpokerbots/class-resources-2025/blob/main/syllabus.pdf); [mitpokerbots repositories](https://github.com/orgs/mitpokerbots/repositories)
- abh-win built a rules-exact local engine, baseline bots (TAG, station, nit, maniac, random, all-in), and reported BB/100 with 95% CIs over 40,000 hands per cell plus an adapter to the official engine — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)
- GTO Wizard ran a separate arena with published benchmarks — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- ACPC used duplicate deals with seat swaps to cut variance; "statistically significant results" are "a tricky issue" — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-texas-hold-em-tournament-for-ais)
- Sequoia starter kit includes a local simulator and ICM calculator — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)

(f) Language choice
- MIT: Python/Java/C++ skeletons every year; strong public entries in 2017 and 2024 used C++ for the runtime with Python for table generation — [SPAM_pokerbot_2017](https://github.com/sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017); [abh-win](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot); [gtowizard](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- Sequoia and the CMU DSC event are Python-only; IIT Pokerbots 2026 allowed only Python — [Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html); [cmudsc.com](https://cmudsc.com/pokerai-2026/); [IIT Pokerbots](https://www.iitpokerbots.in/)

### Inferences
- In a 1 ms Python regime, "equity" must mean a preflop lookup (169 canonical hands vs random / vs a range) plus cheap postflop proxies (made-hand category, draw flags), not Monte Carlo; abh-win's 170 µs per 1,500-sample estimate was C++.
- The two MIT failure modes called out by organizers and participants, engine integration bugs and clock exhaustion, both become fold-on-error under Sequoia rules; wrapping every decision in a try/except that returns a legal check/fold is the cheapest insurance.
- Expect the Sequoia field to contain many all-in/maniac and passive bots in T1–T2 (teams shipping something in 60 minutes); a tight value-heavy bot that calls shoves wide only with premium hands and never bluffs stations should harvest those tables.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how often MIT bots timed out or crashed in finals.
- No public data on Sequoia's starter kit contents beyond the rules' list.

## Key Question 5: Recorded cases of a simple bot beating fancier CFR bots in a short tournament, and why

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous head-to-head record, but several credible accounts show simple heuristic bots beating or outscoring CFR entries in short, student-scale events: MIT organizers say heuristic exploiters win most often and an all-in bot was once nearly uncounterable; a 2017 MIT prize winner with win-rate tables tuned specifically against CFR bots' tendency to fold to big bets; and multiple hobbyist reports of undertrained CFR collapsing to degenerate strategies. The common cause is that CFR needs training time, a faithful abstraction of the variant, and off-tree handling, none of which fit a month, let alone an hour.

### Cited Findings
- MIT organizers (2024): the best performers are bots "that exploit opponent weaknesses, especially those using the most poker theory and heuristics"; a past all-in bot "was hard to counter in real time" — [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2024/02/08/pokerbots)
- 2017 MIT prize-winning table-lookup bot adjusted to CFR opponents by betting smaller because they "tended to fold to large raises" — [SPAM_pokerbot_2017](https://github.com/sebLopezCot/SPAM_pokerbot_2017)
- Hobbyist CFR trained briefly "had learned exactly one move: call" and "a super-simple opponent can take money off it"; the author used a transparent equity heuristic as the training opponent — [playlikeaquant.com](https://www.playlikeaquant.com/all-experiments/my-laptop-played-poker)
- Simplified hold'em CFR project: more iterations help, but the most-trained version "does not win against the random bot by a significant amount" — [ArmanMielke/simple-poker-cfr](https://github.com/ArmanMielke/simple-poker-cfr)
- GTO Wizard (a professional solver team) abandoned a learned value function for an equity heuristic at the depth limit because of the clock, and admits the heuristic is weak — [gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024](https://github.com/gtowizard-ai/mitpoker-2024)
- The 2017 MIT champion's CFR bot needed explicit off-tree error handling and state abstraction to work at all — [Fro116/Pokerbots](https://github.com/Fro116/Pokerbots) (2017)
- abh-win's simple profiled equity bot won all 120 matches vs fixed baselines and beat its own non-profiling variant head-to-head by +132 over 30 matches — [abh-win/auction-poker-bot](https://github.com/abh-win/auction-poker-bot)
- The equity-threshold style's known weakness is exploitability by observant opponents — [Medium, coaching an LM to play poker](https://medium.com/@jay_learner/coaching-a-language-model-to-play-poker-part-1-adding-mathematical-precision-23201030bf38)

### Inferences
- In a 60-minute window, CFR is only viable as a pre-trained artifact a team carries in (allowed: "publicly available documentation" is permitted, but the variant is confidential until build start, so any carried-in blueprint will be off-variant and off-stack-depth). A tight-aggressive, ICM-aware, stats-adjusting bot is the realistic ceiling and, per the MIT evidence, often the winning one in short student fields.
- The all-in-bot anecdote matters more in an 8-max SNG with rising blinds: shove-heavy bots are strong when stacks are short, and the counter (call shoves with a precomputed range vs an inferred shove frequency) is cheap to implement.

### Gaps
- No competition publishes per-entry architecture plus placement, so "simple beat CFR" cannot be quantified from primary data; the above are self-reports and organizer impressions.
- Nothing was found on Theta Poker, Codingame, Kaggle, Berkeley or Stanford pokerbot contests in 2018–2026; the only other active student contests found were IIT Pokerbots (India, Python-only, round-robin prelims, 2,000+ registrations, 820M+ hands in 2026) — [IIT Pokerbots](https://www.iitpokerbots.in/) — and the IIIT Bangalore and Build4Good MIT-engine forks noted above.

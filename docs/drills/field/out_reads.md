# Team notebook — rival_bot

## What it is
`rival_bot.py` (generated by `build.py` from `bot_core.py` + preflop tables in `pf.json`; stdlib only, ~67 KB, import ~0.2 s).

- **Evaluator**: rank-multiset dict (base-5 key, all 2..7-card multisets, 76k entries built at import) + 13-bit flush
  table + suit-count table. ~0.6 µs per 7-card eval in CPython 3.12.
- **Preflop tables** (`precomp.py`, MC offline): 169-class ranking (blend of HU and 4-way equity), equity of every
  class vs top-p ranges for 17 values of p (3k samples each).
- **Utility = ICM**: every decision compares outcomes as Malmuth-Harville expected points (others assumed equal
  stacks, O(n) recursion; busting = points of the current last place). Chip EV is never used directly.
- **Opponent model per seat** (reset per table), fed by a replay of `obs.history` (and `hand_end` history when
  given; blind posts auto-detected): VPIP, PFR, open rate, 3-bet rate, fold to steal, fold to 3-bet, call vs
  all-in, postflop bet rate when checked to, fold/call/raise vs bets, fold to raise, showdown evidence
  (bluffs shown after aggression, air shown after calling). All with Beta-style priors (weight 4-6 hands).
- **Ranges**: each opponent's preflop actions this hand map to a top-p range from his own stats (open/3b/4b/
  limp/call, position-scaled); postflop actions weight sampled hands by a likelihood per hand bucket
  (air / draw / weak pair / top pair+overpair / strong) using his bluff and float estimates; bluff likelihood
  is discounted on later streets and for big bets unless he has been shown bluffing.
- **Postflop**: time-boxed MC (deadline 230 µs from act start, hard stop 400 µs) gives equity vs current range,
  vs calling range, vs raise-continuing range, and per-opponent call probabilities. Candidate actions (check,
  bets of 1/3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.4 pot / fold, call, raise 2.8x, jam) scored in ICM points with fold equity =
  mix of observed fold stat and range-based fold share (price-adjusted for raises).
- **Preflop**: open thresholds by players behind (widened vs blinds that fold to steals, tightened vs 3-bettors);
  push/fold below ~13.5 bb with ICM EV using each opponent's call-vs-all-in rate; 3-bet/4-bet value thresholds
  relative to the raiser's estimated range; calls by pot odds x realization; all-in calls by ICM.

## Test kit
- `sim.py` (full NLHE SNG engine, side pots, ICM at cap) + `sparring.py` bots: tight, lp, maniac, pushfold,
  random, callbot, tag2 (built by a helper agent; tests in test_eval.py / test_sim.py).
- `bench.py <module> <tables> <seed> <fields>`: A = mixed weak field, C = us + 7 tag2, D = 2 tag2 + 2 tight +
  pushfold + maniac + lp, S = us + copy of rival_bot + strong-ish field.
- `diag.py`, `statcheck.py`, `biglose.py`, `dist.py`: timing per street, model stats, big losing hands, finish
  distribution.

Leak finder (`leaks.py`): for each postflop decision, chips won from that point to the end of the hand, in
pots, by street / action / equity bucket. Used to find the turn-bluff and raise-bluff leaks above.

Versus 7 copies of one sparring bot (v17, 300-500 tables): tight 7.33, lp 8.01, maniac 7.41, pushfold 6.93.
Self-play check: v6 in a field of 7 v17 scores 3.88, v17 in a field of 7 v6 scores 4.16 (avg 4.0).

Heads-up from 100 bb (v12, 300 matches each): beats tight 94%, tag2 87%, maniac 60%, pushfold 55%.

SE ~0.10-0.13 at 800-1000 tables; same seed across variants in a row, so differences of <0.25 are noise.

## Timeouts are the biggest unknown
With the sim enforcing 1 ms on this overloaded box, v10 dropped from 6.91 to 6.40 (field A) and 5.88 to 5.72
(field C): ~1% of our decisions get preempted for several ms (the bots that take 5 µs lose ~0.05%). On a
quiet tournament machine this disappears. Compromise taken: MC deadline 200 µs (hard stop 350 µs), and
`gc.freeze()` after import so collections don't rescan the 76k-entry tables.

## Timing
On this loaded box (load avg 11-14 on 8 cores) wall-clock spikes of several ms hit every bot (callbot too);
thread CPU time shows the same spikes, so it is steal time, not our code. Our own p50 is ~15 µs preflop and
~245 µs postflop (MC is time-boxed by wall clock, so slow machines get fewer samples, not timeouts).

## What I would improve next
1. **Turn calls vs value-heavy regs** still lose (leak finder vs tag2: turn call with e<0.45 = -0.25 pot).
   v23 now classifies his hand on the board as it was when he acted, but the bucket model still lumps top
   pair weak kicker with overpairs (tier 2); split tier 2 by kicker/overpair and give made-hand strength
   within tier 3 (sets vs bottom two pair) its own likelihoods.
2. **Future-game / blind pressure**: utility is static ICM. With this flat 10/7/5/4/3/2/1/0 payout, ICM says a
   flip at 8-handed needs ~66% equity, so we fold a lot short-stacked. A short FGS (one orbit look-ahead with
   blinds) would tell us when nitting into the blinds costs more than ICM thinks. Widening push/fold by hand
   (16.5 bb zone) measured worse, so it needs the real model, not a knob.
3. **Timeouts**: on a loaded box ~1% of our decisions get preempted (we sit in act() ~220 µs postflop vs 5 µs
   for the simple bots). If the event box is loaded, a 120 µs budget trades ~0.2 points of strength for ~40%
   fewer timeouts; I left 200 µs. Better: more equity per µs (reuse each sampled opponent hand for 2-3 runouts,
   exact enumeration of the range on the river).
4. **Sizing tells**: bet size is chosen by EV per hand, so bluffs and value can size differently. Adaptive
   opponents could read that. Mix sizes per bucket or pick one size per (street, board texture).
5. **Better sparring**: tag2 is the strongest sparring bot; we need an ICM-aware adaptive opponent (or more
   variants of our own bot with different parameters) to tune against strong fields, since most remaining
   knob changes are inside the ~0.2 same-seed noise.
6. Preflop tables: 3k samples per (class, range) cell; recompute with 30k+ and add multiway-vs-range tables.
